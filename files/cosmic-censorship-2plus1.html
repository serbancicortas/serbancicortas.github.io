Weak Cosmic Censorship in 2+1 dimensions

Penrose's Weak Cosmic Censorship conjecture states that, for generic initial data, singularities are hidden inside black holes. This paper establishes the conjecture for the circularly symmetric Einstein-scalar field system in 2+1 dimensions with \(\Lambda<0\), in the \(C^k\) initial data topology, for any \(k\geq2\). An essential step of the proof is establishing the presence of a mass gap. In particular, this implies that all naked singularities have infinite blueshift, which represents the main instability mechanism.

1.Weak Cosmic Censorship

One of the main open problems in general relativity is the Weak Cosmic Censorship conjecture, proposed by Penrose in 1969 [Pen69]. It states that, for generic initial data, naked singularities do not form. That is to say, singularities are generically hidden inside black holes and invisible to far-away observers who exist for all time: null infinity \(\mathcal I\) is future complete [GH78, Chr99b]. The conjecture is motivated by the fact that a future incomplete \(\mathcal I\) would threaten the predictability of general relativity as a physical theory.

The paper defines naked singularity spacetimes according to the Penrose diagram in Figure 3(d). In particular, every spacetime with a future incomplete \(\mathcal I\) is a naked singularity spacetime.

Christodoulou proved a suitable formulation of the conjecture for the model problem of the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar field system in 3+1 dimensions. The argument of [Chr99a] relies on a novel blueshift effect to show that, while explicit naked singularity spacetimes can be constructed [Chr94, Cho93], all naked singularities are in fact unstable to black hole formation and the set of naked singularity spacetimes is indeed non-generic in a suitable sense. However, the instability mechanism in [Chr99a] required working in a topology defined by a low-regularity class of solutions. Thus, the question of Weak Cosmic Censorship in a smoother topology remains open even in this setting.

This paper initiates the mathematical study of gravitational collapse for the circularly symmetric Einstein-scalar field system in 2+1 dimensions with \(\Lambda<0\), and proves Weak Cosmic Censorship in a topology of arbitrary smoothness: in \(C^k\), for any \(k\ge2\).

2.The model for gravity in 2+1 dimensions

The paper considers the Einstein-scalar field system with a negative cosmological constant:

\[\mathrm{Ric}_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12 R\,g_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_\mu\phi\,\nabla_\nu\phi-\tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}\lvert\nabla\phi\rvert^2,\qquad \square_g\phi=0.\]

In 2+1 dimensions this is perhaps the most elementary model for gravitational collapse that allows for the dynamical formation of singularities [PC00, JGC15]. It was first studied in numerical relativity by Pretorius and Choptuik [PC00].

The simplest solution is anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetime, with \(\phi=0\) and

\[g_{\mathrm{AdS}}=-\big(1+\lvert\Lambda\rvert r^2\big)dt^2+\big(1+\lvert\Lambda\rvert r^2\big)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\sigma_1^2.\]

It is circularly symmetric, contains no singularities, is geodesically complete, and has a timelike asymptotic boundary, null infinity \(\mathcal I\).

The paper studies circularly symmetric solutions, written in global double null coordinates as

\[g=-\Omega^2(u,v)\,du\,dv+r^2(u,v)\,d\sigma_1^2,\qquad \phi=\phi(u,v).\]

We consider maximal developments of \(C^k\) asymptotically AdS characteristic data on an outgoing null cone \(C_0^+\), obtained by solving an initial-boundary value problem with reflective boundary conditions at \(\mathcal I\), adapting the 3+1-dimensional theory of Holzegel–Smulevici [HS12].

Gravitational collapse. The paper only considers solutions with a regular center \(\Gamma=\{r=0\}\). AdS spacetime itself is in this class.

The BTZ family

The Bañados–Teitelboim–Zanelli (BTZ) spacetimes [BTZ92] are a 1-parameter family of circularly symmetric static solutions with \(M\geq0\):

\[g_M=-\big(1-M+\lvert\Lambda\rvert r^2\big)dt^2+\big(1-M+\lvert\Lambda\rvert r^2\big)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\sigma_1^2,\quad \phi_M=0.\]

For \(M=0\) this is AdS. For \(M\in(0,1)\) the metric has a conical singularity at \(r=0\), and for \(M=1\) a cusp singularity. For \(M>1\) the spacetime has no center, but it extends to a two-ended black hole. See the survey [Car95] for more on these solutions.

The paper translates the mass parameter by 1 relative to [BTZ92], so that AdS has \(M=0\) rather than \(M=-1\).

Since the paper only considers spacetimes with a regular center, the only member of the BTZ family in this class is AdS. Nonetheless, the entire BTZ family plays an important role in the asymptotic behavior of the solutions at \(\mathcal I\). It also already exhibits the threshold \(M=1\), which reappears as the mass gap.

3.Global structure of spacetimes

The moduli space \(\mathfrak M\) consists of \(C^k\) asymptotically AdS characteristic data on \(C_0^+\). To each data set we associate its unique maximal development. Adapting the work of Kommemi [Kom13] and of Holzegel–Smulevici [HS12] to 2+1 dimensions, the paper shows that its Penrose diagram is one of the following four.

The spacetimes (b) and (d) are locally naked singularity spacetimes. Light from points arbitrarily close to \(b_\Gamma\) reaches the observers near \(\mathcal B_0\). Equivalently, locally naked singularity spacetimes contain no trapped surfaces in a neighborhood of \(b_\Gamma\). We denote the corresponding set of initial data by \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{loc.naked}}\).

We denote by \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{black}}\subset\mathfrak M\) the set of initial data whose maximal development is a black hole spacetime. Equivalently, the maximal development contains a trapped surface. Since having a trapped surface is an open condition, \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{black}}\) is open.

4.The main result

Theorem 1.1 · Weak Cosmic Censorship

For any \(k\ge2\), the maximal development of generic \(C^k\) asymptotically AdS characteristic data does not contain naked singularities.

In particular, naked singularity spacetimes are non-generic and are unstable to black hole formation. Additionally, locally naked singularities are unstable to the formation of spacelike-only singularities.

Precise statement

More precisely, \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{generic}}:=\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{black}}\sqcup\mathrm{int}\big(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{non}}\big)\) is open and dense in \(\mathfrak M\), and:

\[\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{naked}}\subset\mathrm{cl}\big(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{black}}\big)\setminus\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{black}}\subset\mathfrak M\setminus\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{generic}}\] \[\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{loc.naked}}\subset\mathrm{cl}\big(\mathfrak M^{\mathrm{spacelike}}_{\mathrm{black}}\big)\setminus\mathfrak M^{\mathrm{spacelike}}_{\mathrm{black}}\]

The paper chooses a more elementary notion of genericity than Christodoulou [Chr99a]. It proves that naked singularity data lie in the complement of an open and dense subset of \(\mathfrak M\), rather than that they have positive codimension. The reason is the need to extend initial data perturbations globally and satisfy the boundary conditions at \(\mathcal I\).

Numerical relativity works on critical collapse ([Cho93] in 3+1 dimensions, see also the review [GMG07]; [PC00] and [JGC15] in 2+1 dimensions) suggest the above conjectural picture of the moduli space. Take an initial data profile \(\Phi\) and any scaling parameter \(p\). For small \(p\) the solution does not collapse, for large \(p\) it forms a black hole, and at the threshold \(p=p^*\) it is expected to form a naked singularity. In this picture the threshold is a codimension-1 set of naked singularity spacetimes, consistent with Theorem 1.1. The theorem also covers naked singularities off the threshold, if they exist, showing they are surrounded by \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{black}}\).

5.Idea of the proof

The overall strategy goes back to Christodoulou [Chr99a]. The main step is to show that locally naked singularities are unstable to the formation of spacelike-only singularities, and for this it suffices to perturb them so that trapped surfaces form arbitrarily close to \(b_\Gamma\). The proof relies on two fundamental results about circularly symmetric solutions: a mass gap, and a quantitative criterion for the formation of trapped surfaces. Together they show that every first singularity at the center has unbounded blueshift, and the blueshift instability then produces the trapped surfaces.

5.1.The mass gap

The paper defines the Hawking mass by

\[m=-g(\nabla r,\nabla r)-\Lambda r^2+1,\]

translated by 1 as for the BTZ family. It is non-negative for solutions arising in gravitational collapse, and it vanishes on a circle exactly when the solution is AdS in the domain of dependence of the disk bounded by that circle.

A circle is trapped exactly when \(m>1+\lvert\Lambda\rvert r^2\).

The Bondi mass is \(M=\lim_{r\to\infty}m|_{C_0^+}\), and the reflective boundary conditions are \(m|_{\mathcal I}=M\).

Theorem 1.2 · Mass gap

No singularities form in the domain of dependence of a disk bounded by a circle with Hawking mass strictly less than 1.

Proof idea

For the proof of Theorem 1.2, the bound on \(m\) first gives quantitative control of the geometry. A non-concentration estimate then shows that the scalar field cannot blow up at a faster than self-similar rate, so \(r\partial_r\phi\) is bounded. This improves by a small power of \(r\): \(r^{1-\rho}\partial_r\phi\) is bounded for some small \(\rho>0\). These bounds propagate the local well-posedness norm from the initial data, so no singularities form.

The main use of the mass gap is the following description of first singularities at the center:

Corollary 1.1

Let \(b_\Gamma=(u_*,v_*)\) be a first singularity at the center \(\Gamma\). Then \(m(u,v_*)\to1^+\) as \(u\to u_*^-\).

Globally, the Hawking mass is non-decreasing along \(C_0^+\) and tends to the Bondi mass at \(\mathcal I\). As a corollary, we obtain the mass gap in terms of the Bondi mass:

Corollary 1.2

Solutions with Bondi mass less than 1 do not form singularities. More precisely, if \(\mathfrak D\in\mathfrak M\) has \(M<1\), then \(\mathfrak D\in\mathrm{int}\big(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{non}}\big)\). In particular, small perturbations of AdS spacetime are non-collapsing.

This proves a conjecture of Bizoń and Jałmużna [BJ13], who gave numerical evidence for very small Bondi mass. It marks a sharp contrast with AdS spacetime in 3+1 dimensions, which is unstable to black hole formation, see [DH06, BR11, Mos20, Mos23].

5.2.Formation of trapped surfaces criterion

The second ingredient is a quantitative criterion for trapped surface formation, inspired by Christodoulou's work in 3+1 dimensions [Chr91]. Intuitively, if the energy content of an annular region bounded by two circles on the outgoing null cone \(C_0^+\) is large compared to the size of the region, then a trapped surface forms in evolution.

Theorem 1.3 · Formation of trapped surfaces

Let \(C_1=\{v=v_1\}\) be an ingoing cone along which \(r\to0\) and \(m\ge1\), and let \(C_2=\{v=v_2\}\) with \(v_2>v_1\). On \(C_0^+\) set

\[\delta_0=\frac{r(u_0,v_2)}{r(u_0,v_1)}-1,\qquad \eta_0=\frac{m(u_0,v_2)-m(u_0,v_1)}{\lvert\Lambda\rvert\,r^2(u_0,v_2)}.\]

If \(\delta_0>0\) is small enough and \(\eta_0>\delta_0\), then a marginally trapped surface forms on \(C_2\), at radius at least \(\frac{5\delta_0/4}{1+\delta_0}\,r(u_0,v_2)\).

Proof idea

The proof adapts Christodoulou's argument [Chr91] to 2+1 dimensions: the monotonicity properties of the system give a differential inequality for \(\eta=\lvert\Lambda\rvert^{-1}r^{-2}(u,v_2)\big(m(u,v_2)-m(u,v_1)\big)\) along \(C_2\).

Following Christodoulou [Chr99a], the criterion is used in two ways: directly, after the perturbation, and through its contrapositive, to get a priori control of the scalar field for any locally naked singularity spacetime.

5.3.Locally naked singularity spacetimes

Let \(b_\Gamma\) be a first singularity at the center whose past light cone \(C_0^-\) intersects \(C_0^+\), and choose double null coordinates such that

\[b_\Gamma=(0,0),\qquad C_0^-=\{v=0,\ 2r=-u\},\qquad C_0^+=\{u=u_0,\ 2r=v-u_0\}.\]

The renormalized outgoing derivative of the scalar field \(\theta=\sqrt r\,\partial_v\phi/\partial_vr\) satisfies

\[\partial_u\theta=\frac{2\lvert\Lambda\rvert r(-\partial_ur)}{1+\lvert\Lambda\rvert r^2-m}\,\theta-\frac{1}{2\sqrt r}\,\partial_u\phi,\]

and the blueshift is the integrating factor of this equation:

\[\mathbf b(u,v)=\int_{u_0}^{u}\frac{2\lvert\Lambda\rvert r(-\partial_ur)}{1+\lvert\Lambda\rvert r^2-m}(u',v)\,du',\qquad \mathbf b(u)=\mathbf b(u,0).\]

Near \(C_0^-\) the mass gap gives \(m\ge1\), and the absence of trapped surfaces gives \(m\le1+\lvert\Lambda\rvert r^2\). Thus, we get Proposition 5.1:

\[\mathbf b(u)\ \ge\ 2\log\frac{u_0}{u}.\]

So all first singularities at the center have infinite blueshift, whether or not they are locally naked.

Moreover, the absence of trapped surfaces near \(b_\Gamma\) gives, by the contrapositive of Theorem 1.3, bounds for the energy of the scalar field in a region \(\mathcal P\) near \(b_\Gamma\), whose size depends on the strength of the blueshift, proving Proposition 5.2:

\[\int_0^v\theta^2(u,v')\,dv'\lesssim\frac{v\lvert u\rvert}{(1+\lvert\Lambda\rvert r^2-m)(u,0)}.\]

In \(\mathcal P\) one also has \(e^{\mathbf b(u,v)}\sim e^{\mathbf b(u)}\) and \(\partial_vr(u,v)\sim e^{-\mathbf b(u)}\).

5.4.The blueshift instability

Consider perturbations of the initial data on \(C_0^+\) supported on \(\{v\ge0\}\), so that they leave the causal past of \(b_\Gamma\) unchanged. For any integer \(n>k+1\) and any \(\lambda\ne0\), set

\[r_\lambda=r,\ \ \phi_\lambda=\phi\ \text{ for } v\le0,\qquad r_\lambda=r,\ \ \theta_\lambda=\theta+\lambda v^n\ \text{ for } 0\le v\le v_0'.\]

The main role of the blueshift is to amplify this perturbation: the main contribution in \(\theta_\lambda-\theta\) comes from the integrating factor acting on \(\lambda v^n\),

\[\int_0^v\Big\lvert(\theta_\lambda-\theta)(u,v')-e^{\mathbf b_\lambda(u,v')}(\theta_\lambda-\theta)(u_0,v')\Big\rvert^2dv'\ \le\ \lambda^2v^{2n+\frac43}e^{2\mathbf b(u)}\ \ll\ \lambda^2v^{2n+1}e^{2\mathbf b(u)}.\]

Concretely, along the curve \(\mathcal C_\lambda=\{(u,v_\sharp(u))\}\) the two estimates above give

\[\int_0^{v_\sharp(u)}\big\lvert\theta_\lambda(u,v')\big\rvert^2dv'\gtrsim\lambda^2\big[v_\sharp(u)\big]^{2n+1}e^{2\mathbf b(u)},\]

which implies \(\eta_\lambda\big(u,v_\sharp(u)\big)>\delta_\lambda\big(u,v_\sharp(u)\big)\) for all \(\lvert u\rvert\) small. So the perturbed spacetime contains a sequence of trapped surfaces converging to \(b_\Gamma\) (Proposition 6.1), and its singularity is spacelike-only.

Finally, the perturbation is constructed only on a compact subset of \(C_0^+\). For \(\lambda\) small, Appendix A extends it to asymptotically AdS data, a nontrivial step needed because of the timelike boundary \(\mathcal I\). Cauchy stability and the decomposition of \(\mathfrak M\) complete the proof of Theorem 1.1.

Both proofs use the formation of trapped surfaces criterion and its contrapositive, and both rely on the fact that locally naked singularity spacetimes have unbounded blueshift. In Christodoulou's theorem [Chr99a] the low-regularity topology is necessary to activate the blueshift instability. Here, the different scaling of the problem and the quantitative lower bound \(\mathbf b(u)\ge2\log(u_0/u)\) allow the blueshift instability to work in \(C^k\).

The two approaches also differ. Christodoulou derives refined necessary conditions for a spacetime to be a locally naked singularity, as compatibility conditions between the data on \(C_0^+\) and the solution on \(C_0^-\), and then violates them by perturbing the initial data. This paper starts with the perturbation and carries out most of the argument in the perturbed spacetime, describing in detail how the solution enters the regime where Theorem 1.3 applies.

7.Upcoming work

Constructing examples of naked singularities plays a fundamental role in understanding their general properties and their instability. Moreover, the existence of such examples justifies the need to include genericity conditions in any formulation of the Weak Cosmic Censorship conjecture.

In 3+1 dimensions, Choptuik's numerical work on critical collapse [Cho93] suggests the existence of naked singularities on the threshold between black hole formation and non-collapse. This threshold behavior has never been constructed rigorously. The examples of naked singularities that do exist, by Christodoulou [Chr94] for the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar field system and by Rodnianski and Shlapentokh-Rothman [RSR23, SR22] for the Einstein vacuum equations, are not proved to be on the threshold, and they arise from low-regularity initial data.

For the Einstein-dust system (Christodoulou [Chr84]) and the Einstein–Euler system (Guo, Hadžić and Jang [GHJ23]), there are naked singularities arising from smooth data, but these matter models are prone to singularity formation even in the absence of gravity.

In 2+1 dimensions with \(\Lambda<0\), the numerical works of Pretorius–Choptuik [PC00] and Jałmużna–Gundlach–Chmaj [JGC15] see the same threshold behavior.

The first threshold examples. In upcoming work with Igor Rodnianski [CR26], we give the first rigorous construction of naked singularities on the threshold of gravitational collapse: locally naked singularities arising in (local) critical collapse. Unlike all previous naked singularity examples for scalar field models, our examples arise from high-regularity data, as the numerics predict.

On the finite outgoing cone \(C_0^+=\{u=u_0,\ 0\le r<r_0\}\), we construct a 2-parameter family of \(C^3\) characteristic data \(\mathfrak D^q_\lambda\), for any integer \(q\ge4\) and \(\lambda\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\), with the following properties:

  • For \(\lambda<0\), the development of \(\mathfrak D^q_\lambda\) does not form singularities.
  • For \(\lambda=0\), the development is a locally naked singularity spacetime and contains no trapped surfaces. The case \((\lambda,q)=(0,4)\) corresponds to the numerical examples of [PC00, JGC15].
  • For \(\lambda>0\), the development contains trapped surfaces.

For \(\lambda=0\) the blueshift has the exact rate

\[\mathbf b(u)=\Big(3-\frac1{2q}\Big)\log\frac{u_0}{u}+O(1),\]

which should be compared with the general lower bound \(2\log(u_0/u)\) of Proposition 5.1.

An immediate consequence is that \(\mathfrak D^q_0\) extends to asymptotically AdS data in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{loc.naked}}\). In particular \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{loc.naked}}\neq\emptyset\): locally naked singularities do occur in the class of solutions of this paper, and by Theorem 1.1 each of them is unstable to the formation of spacelike-only singularities.
